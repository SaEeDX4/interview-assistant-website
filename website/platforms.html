Platforms & roadmap

Windows first. Android built. iOS and Mac planned — as native apps.

One platform is the focus today: Windows. The Android app is already built — a full mirror of the Windows app — and waits only on publication. iOS and Mac are decided native apps that follow it. We would rather tell you exactly where each platform stands than promise a date we cannot keep.

Windows — first platform, installer pending

The platform the app is built on. WASAPI loopback from your headset.

Android — built, not yet published

The full app, mirroring Windows — with the interview on the same phone as its headline mode. Publication has not happened yet.

iOS — planned

A native app that follows Android, designed within iOS’s known platform limits. No date, no TestFlight promise.

Mac — planned

Dedicated native app, not a port. No date.

Windows — first platform

Windows 10 / 11, 64-bit

The platform the app is built on. The desktop app captures the interviewer’s audio from your headset via WASAPI loopback — the Windows audio path this product was built on. Preparation, live assist, Coach Mode, reports, and the 10-language support all live here.

The signed installer is not published yet — see Download.

Built — not yet published

Android — the full app, built

The Android app is built: a full mirror of the Windows app — the same features, the same question bank, the same Coach Mode, the same honest report. Its headline mode runs the interview on the same phone: the meeting plays on the phone and the app hears it there. Where a meeting app blocks that capture, a microphone mode remains as the automatic fallback.

It is not published yet: there is no APK to download, and none will be offered until the release is real. No date. No waitlist yet — the real waitlist will name its processor and privacy policy at launch.

Planned

iOS — planned native app

iOS follows Android, as its own native app. Its platform limits are known, not open questions: iOS offers no WASAPI-style capture of another app’s call audio, no global hotkeys, and distribution is App Store only — the app is designed around those limits from the start, rather than around promises the platform cannot keep.

No date, and no TestFlight promise.

Planned

Mac — dedicated native app, planned

A dedicated native Mac app is planned — not a port, a native app built for macOS in its own right.

No date.

Roadmap — five gated phases

The plan, with gates — not dates

Each phase has pass/fail exit criteria and pause criteria. A phase does not start until the one before it has evidence. No phase carries a calendar promise.

  1. Phase 1 — Documentation v2. The planning package behind this site: 71 documents, an audit, and a website checklist. Done
  2. Phase 2 — Website and Windows commercial launch. This site, the signed Windows installer, live Paddle checkout tested end to end, and final legal and support pages. In progress
  3. Phase 3 — Android publication. The app itself is built — a full mirror of Windows, with the same-phone interview as its headline mode. What remains is publication: the Google Play path and the signed direct download, each gated on the launch phase. App built — publication pending
  4. Phase 4 — iOS and native Mac apps. Native apps following Android: iOS as its own native app, designed within its platform’s known limits; Mac as a dedicated native app, not a port. Planned
  5. Phase 5 — Growth, support, and product quality. Support, quality, and growth work on top of a launched, tested product. Planned

Full detail lives in the planning docs in the project repository — including the pause/kill criteria for each phase.
